# MedAIx copy guide

Use calm, plain language that helps a person understand what happened and what
they can do next.

## Labels and messages

- Label actions with a specific verb and object, such as “Add a report”.
- Prefer sentence case and three words or fewer when the meaning stays clear.
- Avoid generic labels such as “Submit” or “Learn more”.
- Do not use urgency, scarcity, social proof, exclamation marks, or emoji.
- Add risk reducing microcopy only when it is true for that flow.
- For failures, say what happened, whether the person’s data is safe, then give
  one primary action.

## Action hierarchy

- Give each screen or sheet one filled primary action.
- Use tonal or text actions for secondary choices.
- Keep destructive actions in a confirmation sheet with a clear “Keep” option.
- Keep the button label visible while it is loading and show a spinner.
- Explain a disabled action with a short reason beside the control.
- Show a completed state with a clear success label and icon.

## Reassurance

Use copy such as “Your report stays saved on your account” only where the
underlying flow guarantees it. Never imply a clinical conclusion or replace a
clinician’s advice.
